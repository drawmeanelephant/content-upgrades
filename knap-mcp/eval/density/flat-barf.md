# Should You Use AcmeDB?

AcmeDB is a key-value database that has been gaining attention in the developer community for its focus on durability and simplicity. It stores data using a write-ahead log and a manifest file, which means writes are persisted to disk before they are acknowledged, and the overall design avoids many of the moving parts found in distributed databases. Many developers appreciate that it does not require a dedicated operations team to run, and its documentation is generally considered clear and easy to follow. However, there are some limitations that should be considered before adopting it in production. For example, it currently lacks a managed backup solution, and the documentation lists point-in-time snapshots as a feature that is coming soon. It also only supports a single region, which may be a concern for organizations with strict availability or disaster recovery requirements. Performance is reported to be strong, with the vendor claiming p99 read latencies under two milliseconds, although it is worth noting that these benchmarks were run by the vendor on a laptop and have not been independently verified. The launch post describes the project as intentionally boring, and that seems to be an accurate description of its goals.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, AcmeDB is a promising database that offers a compelling balance of simplicity and durability for teams that do not need the complexity of a distributed system. Its straightforward architecture and helpful documentation make it easy to get started, and its reported performance numbers are impressive. That said, the lack of managed backups and the single-region limitation mean that it may not be the right choice for every workload, especially those that handle critical data or require high availability across multiple regions. Ultimately, whether you should use AcmeDB depends on your specific needs and risk tolerance. If you are building a cache, a prototype, or a service where occasional data loss would be acceptable, it could be a great fit. If you need enterprise-grade reliability, you may want to wait until the backup story matures or consider a more established alternative. As always, it is important to evaluate any technology carefully before adopting it.
